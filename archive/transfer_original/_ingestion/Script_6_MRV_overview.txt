On thing to note how they defined quantifiable uncertianty - how did thye do this and how does it compare?
This is actually the most important methodological distinction between what the paper did and what we are currently proposing.

Most DSM studies claim they have "uncertainty" because they produce a standard deviation, confidence interval, or ensemble spread. Wadoux et al. argue that this is not sufficient for MRV.

Three Levels of Uncertainty
Think of uncertainty in three nested levels:

Level 1: Point Prediction Uncertainty
For a pixel:

Plain Text


SOC = 35 Mg C ha⁻¹
SD = 8 Mg C ha⁻¹
This tells you the uncertainty at that specific location.

Most machine learning DSM papers stop here.

Examples:

Random Forest variance
Quantile Regression Forest intervals
Ensemble spread
Bayesian posterior SD
This is what your Bayesian model naturally produces.

Level 2: Map-Level Uncertainty
You predict:

Plain Text


SOC Mean Raster
SOC SD Raster
across the AOI.

Again, many studies stop here.

The hidden assumption is:

Every pixel uncertainty is independent.

That assumption is almost always wrong.

Level 3: Spatially Aggregated Uncertainty
This is the paper's big contribution.

Suppose we have:

Plain Text


1000 cells
each with:

Plain Text


Mean = 35
SD = 8
If errors were independent:

R


SE_mean = SD / sqrt(n)
and uncertainty collapses quickly.

But soil isn't independent spatially.

If one ridge is overpredicted:

Plain Text


Nearby cells
also overpredicted
because:

same covariates
same environmental conditions
same model behaviour
Errors become spatially autocorrelated.

The authors explicitly modelled this.

The Really Interesting Part for Additional Sampling
This is where the paper becomes directly useful for your question:

How much would new samples improve the map?

They quantified this by repeatedly retraining models with progressively larger sample sizes.

For each sample size they calculated:

Plain Text


RMSE
Coverage
Project-level uncertainty
The surprising result was:

Mean SOC changed very little
Even with much fewer samples.

Meaning:

Plain Text


30 Mg/ha
31 Mg/ha
29 Mg/ha
all close.

Uncertainty changed a lot
Example conceptually:

Samples	Mean SOC	Project SD
10	30	5.0
20	30	3.5
30	30	2.7
40	30	2.3
50	30	2.1
The estimate stabilizes early.

The confidence improves later.

This is probably exactly what you'll find with your community carbon datasets.

My prediction for your workflow is that additional cores will not dramatically change the AOI-wide mean carbon stock, but they will substantially reduce uncertainty and improve the ability to detect future changes. From an MRV perspective, that reduction in uncertainty is often more valuable than a small change in the mean estimate itself.
